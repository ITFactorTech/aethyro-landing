How to Evaluate an AI Tool Before You Commit

Updated September 24, 2026 6 min read by Aethyro

There are dozens of AI chat tools available right now. Most of them will happily take your $20 a month. Fewer of them will tell you clearly what happens to your conversations, whether they train on your data, or what you actually get for that subscription fee.

Before you commit to any AI service — for your household, your freelance work, or your small business — here are the specific things worth checking. None of this takes more than 15 minutes, and it can save you a lot of frustration later.

1. Read the Privacy Policy (the Right Section)

You don't need to read the whole thing. Search the page for these two phrases: "train" and "improve our models." What you're looking for is whether the company uses your conversations to train future AI models.

OpenAI trains on ChatGPT conversations by default — you can opt out in settings, but it's not opt-in. Many smaller AI tools have no opt-out at all. Anthropic (the company behind Claude) explicitly does not train on API conversations — your inputs go in and your outputs come back, and that's it.

If you're using AI for client work, financial planning, medical questions, or anything sensitive, this distinction matters significantly.

2. Understand the Real Cost Structure

Most AI subscriptions charge a flat monthly fee regardless of usage. If you use the tool heavily every day, that's fine. If you use it sporadically — a few times a week, or heavily in one month and barely at all in the next — you're paying for idle time every month, forever.

Ask yourself: do I use AI consistently enough to justify a $20/month subscription that resets every 30 days whether I use it or not?

For a lot of families and small business owners, the honest answer is no. A credit-based model — where you buy what you need and unused credits carry over — fits actual usage patterns far better. The math usually looks like:

  • Heavy daily user (50+ conversations/month): flat subscription wins
  • Moderate user (15–30 conversations/month): roughly break-even
  • Occasional user (fewer than 15 conversations/month): credits save money every month

3. Test the Model Quality on Your Actual Work

This is the step most people skip. Every AI tool will demo well on generic prompts. What matters is how it performs on the specific things you need it to do.

Run these tests before committing:

  • Paste a real document (a contract, an invoice, a long email thread) and ask for a summary. Does it get the key points right?
  • Ask it to draft something in your voice by giving it a few examples of your writing. Does it approximate your style, or does it sound like a press release?
  • Ask a question it might not know and see if it admits uncertainty or confidently makes something up.
  • Give it a multi-step task — something that requires it to hold context across several instructions. Does it track what you've already said?

That last one is where cheaper or older models fall apart the fastest. Long-context reasoning — maintaining coherence across a 20-turn conversation about a complex project — separates top-tier models from the rest.

4. Check What You Can Actually Do With It

Some AI tools are chat-only. Others let you upload files, process images, work in multiple languages, or connect to external tools. Know what you need before you sign up.

Common things small businesses need that basic AI tools often don't support well:

  • Pasting in long documents (contracts, proposals, research) and getting accurate analysis
  • Drafting client-facing content that actually sounds professional
  • Working through a business problem with follow-up questions across a long conversation
  • Reviewing code, formulas, or technical content without hallucinating

Red Flags vs. Green Flags

  • 🚩 Trains on your conversations by default, with a buried opt-out
  • 🚩 No free trial — you pay before you can test anything
  • 🚩 Monthly subscription with no usage-based option
  • 🚩 Vague or missing privacy policy language around data use
  • 🚩 Confidently wrong answers with no acknowledgment of uncertainty
  • ✅ Explicit no-training policy on your conversations
  • ✅ Free trial with no credit card required
  • ✅ Credits or pay-as-you-go option for occasional users
  • ✅ Clear, specific privacy policy you can actually read
  • ✅ Admits when it doesn't know something
